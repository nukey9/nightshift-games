1. The game

Barricaded is a round-based zombie survival game for Meta Quest 3 and 3S that plays in your real room. The game reads your headset's room scan, turns your doors and windows into barricaded ways in, and opens a ruined world beyond your walls. Zombies come through the windows, break through the walls, and get faster and tougher every round. You hold them off with real-handed guns — loading, racking and reloading them yourself — and spend the points you earn on better weapons, perks and upgrades.

  • Your Room — the room you stand in (or, with Multi-room on, every scanned room joined to it by a door, each bought open COD-style).
  • Outdoor Arena — a fenced arena set out on open ground (6–15 m a side, 12×12 m by default, in four sections you buy open). No room scan needed.
  • Co-op — two players free, up to four when the host has the Supporter Pack, all in the same physical space and on the same Wi-Fi.

Clear your play space of anything you could trip over or hit. See the safety section of our Terms of Service.

2. Before you play

Room scan and permission

For Your Room the headset needs a room scan (Space Setup) and the game needs your Spatial Data permission. The PLAY page's start button tells you what's missing: Allow Room Access (the permission was refused — or turn it on in Settings > Privacy & Safety > App Permissions > Spatial Data), or Scan My Room (opens Space Setup). Capture every wall, door, window, the floor and the ceiling. The Outdoor Arena needs neither.

The menu

Press the menu button on the left controller to open or close it. Tabs: PLAY (start a game, co-op), LOADOUT (the gunsmith: fit attachments, between games), SETTINGS, GRAPHICS, SCORES, ACCOUNT and SUPPORT. During a match SETTINGS has Restart and Stop Game.

Tutorial and practice

The tutorial is offered the first time, and TUTORIAL on the PLAY page replays it: drawing, shooting, reloading, racking, holstering, shoving, boarding a window, buying, and getting back up. You can't be hurt in it. Between matches, a firing range outside your window has one of every enemy standing still to practise on.

3. The core loop

Round after round: the points you earn buy what keeps you alive in the next one.

  1. Start with only the M9A1 pistol. Grenades refill a little each round.
  2. Hold the ways in. Zombies tear the planks off your windows and climb in, or smash through a wall. Every hit pays, and kills pay more.
  3. Board up between attackers: grab a plank from the pile under a broken window and hold it in place for 2 seconds. Planks you nail where a zombie broke in pay too.
  4. Spend early, spend often. A better gun from the wall rack in round 2–3 makes rounds 4–8 far easier. Then roll the mystery box for something better.
  5. Start the generator — nothing else in the power line works without it — then buy perks: Toughness and Firepower first.
  6. Upgrade your best gun at the upgrade breaks (after rounds 9, 19, 29 …).
  7. Survive the specials: crawlers in round 5, bosses in round 10, hellhounds in round 15 …

4. Rounds, breaks and bosses

  • Zombies per round (per player in co-op): 6 in round 1, 8 in round 2, 11, 13, 16 … 28 by round 10. Up to 6 are about at once in round 1, one more each round, never more than 12.
  • They get harder: about 10% more health every round to round 10, then 10% on top of that each round (up to 25×). Their hits grow from 30 to 40 damage by round 20, and they're at full speed by round 15.
  • Short pauses of 5–8 seconds between rounds; a break of up to 2 minutes after every 5th round; an upgrade break of up to 3 minutes after rounds 9, 19, 29 …; and a break before every hellhound round. Any player can press Continue to start the next round early.
RoundWhat comes

6. What to buy

Hold grip on anything for sale to buy it. Prices are the same every round; harder reload settings make everything a little cheaper, and an outdoor arena with sections off costs half again.

The mystery box

Hold grip on the lid: it spins and offers a random weapon, which waits 8 seconds for you to take it. Your first turn each match costs 500, each turn after 250 more, up to 1250. Playing together there's one box; each player pays from their own points, and anyone can grab what it offers.

The odds shift toward the good tiers with every round and every turn you take, and a run of bad luck pushes them further — until you get a rare or very rare. A weapon you've already had this match is less likely to come up again. Every weapon in a tier is equally likely. Gold weapons come with the Supporter Pack; without it they're left out of the draw.

Doors

With Multi-room on, the doorways between your scanned rooms are boarded up, and in the outdoor arena the sections are fenced off. Buy one open with grip: the first costs 500, each after 250 more (shared in co-op). Open rooms bring more ways in — and more room to fight in. A room joins only if your scan has the door between them.

7. Power, perks and upgrades

The generator

Perk machines and the upgrade station need power. Follow the cables to the generator and hold grip on it to start it: 500 points (1500 when it stands right beside you, in an open arena). Playing together, one start powers everyone.

Perks

Hold grip on a machine's button panel, then drink the bottle. Perks last for the match. Where there's no room for separate machines, a perk carousel sells several: turn it with the arrow buttons, then hold grip on Buy.

It opens only during the upgrade break after rounds 9, 19, 29 … (and needs power). Drop a gun in the glowing slot and wait 6 seconds. Upgrade as many guns, as many times, as your points allow — up to level 3 each.

Every level: 15% more damage, 10% faster fire, ammo filled, a camo and a violet muzzle flash (red at level 3). Upgrading a grenade carries one more.

8. Your belt, grenades and drops

Two hands and four slots: up to six guns. Click a thumbstick for a menu of what's on your belt.

  • Holster a gun by letting go of it over a slot. A dropped gun flies back to its slot. Let go over a full slot and the two swap — the old one goes into your hand.
  • Machine guns (M60, MG 34, M249, MG3), the minigun and the flamethrower need both hands.
  • Grenades (MK2): 4 more each round, up to 6, from a chest slot that refills. Pull the pin with your other hand's trigger (or your mouth), throw. One with its pin still in can be put back.
  • Drops: now and then a kill drops something (at most two a round, gone after 30 seconds): Full Ammo refills every gun you carry; from round 6 a flamethrower, from round 11 MAX CHAOS (30 seconds of free fire), from round 15 a minigun. The drop weapons last until they run dry.

9. Going down and reviving

  • You heal 10 health a second after 5 seconds without being hit.
  • Alone: at 0 health it's game over — unless you have Second Wind, which lets you choose to get back up (on half health).
  • Together: you go down and bleed out over 30 seconds. A teammate revives you by holding a hand near your head for about 7 seconds (half that with Field Medic); you're back on half health. Bled out, you're back next round with full health but only your starting pistol and no perks — your points are kept. The match ends when nobody is left standing.
  • Staying within 3 m of a teammate gives you both 10% more damage and healing.

10. Levels, scores and the Supporter Pack

  • XP: 10 a kill, 40 for every round you play from start to finish, and a little for time played. Level 2 at 250 XP, 3 at 750, 4 at 1500, 5 at 2500 … Levels show your experience; they don't lock anything — every gun and attachment in the gunsmith is open from the start.
  • Leaderboards (SCORES): best and all-time scores, for your room and for the outdoor arena, everyone or just friends.
  • Supporter Pack (SUPPORT tab): 14 more mystery box guns (gold above), the Akimbo perks, and co-op for 3–4 players (only the host needs it). Everything else is free. See the FAQ.

11. Playing together

  1. Everyone in the same physical space and on the same Wi-Fi.
  2. One player: PLAY page → co-op box → Host a game here. The host's room becomes everyone's room.
  3. The others: Join the game shown (or accept the host's invite).
  4. Everyone lines up (next section) — usually by itself within a few seconds.
  5. The host starts the match. The host runs the zombies; everyone sees and fights the same ones.

If someone crashes or drops out, they can Rejoin, and the others can choose to go on without them.

12. Lining up headsets

Each headset tracks the room in its own way. To play together, every headset has to agree where the walls, windows and zombies are — that's “lining up”. A headset that isn't lined up sees everything shifted or turned.

What happens by itself

  • The host's shared anchor: the hosting headset marks a spot on its floor and shares it through Meta; your headset finds the same spot. This needs the internet and Enhanced Spatial Services on both headsets (Settings > Privacy & Safety > Device permissions).
  • Your saved line-up: once you've lined up with a host in a room, your headset remembers it. Next time, if the shared anchor can't be had (or takes more than a few seconds), the saved line-up does the job — offline. If the shared anchor turns up later and disagrees, it wins and the saved line-up is replaced.
  • Room scans: when both headsets have scanned the room, the host matches the walls, doors and windows of the two scans.

The co-op box says Lined up with the host when it's done. Check by looking at a window: its frame should sit on the real one.

The buttons are in the PLAY page's co-op box between games, and on SETTINGS during a match. Lining up during a match takes you out of the fight for up to 30 seconds (zombies leave you alone), once a round, and not while you're down, just hurt, or with a zombie close by.

Lining up by QR code

Needs Quest 3 or 3S on Horizon OS v83 or later. Print a code from the line-up code page first.

Step 1

Print a code at actual size (the black square 18 cm across) on plain matte paper.

Step 2

Tape it flat on the floor (where nobody steps) or on a wall at chest height, somewhere well lit. Leave it there.

Step 3 · host

The host presses Read the QR code and looks at it from a step or two away, holding still a moment, until it says QR code read. Once a session is enough.

Step 4 · everyone else

Each friend presses Line up by QR code and looks at the same code, holding still a moment: Lined up by the QR code. The line-up is saved for next time.

The headset reads the code itself; the game is told only where it is and what it says, never a camera image. It needs the game's Spatial Data permission — the game asks for it if it was turned off.

Lining up by touch

Works on any headset, with no internet and no scan. Pick two spots everyone can reach, at least 1 m apart (2–3 m is best) — two corners of a table, or two marks on the floor.

Step 1

Agree on spot A and spot B. Everyone touches the same two, in the same order.

Step 2

The host presses Set the touch spots; each friend presses Line up by touch. With an empty hand, put the controller's tip on spot A and hold the trigger still for a second until it buzzes. Then spot B the same way.

It doesn't matter who goes first. When both have touched, you're lined up (“Lined up with the host by touch”). If the game says your spots are a different distance apart from the host's, you touched different spots — start again at A.

Still out of line?

  • Check both headsets scanned the same room recently (Space Setup), in good light.
  • Try Line up again, then the QR code, then touch.
  • The host can press Realign: everyone lines up again with the host's room as it is now.
  • Still stuck? Tell us — include the headsets' Horizon OS versions.
